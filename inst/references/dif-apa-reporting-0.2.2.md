# DIF/DFF APA reporting boundary for 0.2.2

This note records the source-based reporting decisions behind the 0.2.2
`dif_report(..., style = "apa")`, `apa_table()`, and
`build_apa_outputs(..., dif_results = ...)` workflow.

## Source anchors

- Holland and Thayer (1988) anchor the Mantel-Haenszel DIF route as an
  observed-score item-performance procedure, not a fitted many-facet model.
- Dorans and Holland (1993) anchor the distinction between DIF detection and
  DIF description. In `mfrmr`, that maps to reporting method, matching basis,
  effect metric, and direction rather than collapsing the output into a bias
  verdict.
- Eckes (2015) anchors the many-facet Rasch reporting boundary for
  rater-mediated assessments: facet functioning, fit, and interaction evidence
  support validation review, but do not automatically establish fairness or
  bias conclusions.
- APA Style's current numbers/statistics guidance anchors exact p-value
  reporting conventions, including reporting p values below .001 as
  `p < .001` in narrative text.
- The `difR::difMH()` help surface is used as an implementation-alignment
  check for the MH observed-score statistic, not as a claim that `difR`
  implements MFRM.

## Implementation decisions

1. `dif_report()` keeps its original narrative as the default. The new
   `style = "apa"` branch is opt-in and returns a manuscript-oriented paragraph.
2. Every `mfrm_dif_report` now carries `apa_table`, `apa_caption`, `apa_note`,
   `apa_section`, and `apa_section_text`, so tables and text are generated from
   the same result object.
3. Fitted-model `analyze_dff()` / `analyze_dif()` results are reported as MFRM
   DFF/DIF screening. The APA paragraph names the facet, grouping variable,
   method, contrast count, strongest finite contrast, test statistic, raw p
   value, adjusted p value, and classification.
4. `analyze_dif_mh()` results are reported as observed-score
   Mantel-Haenszel DIF screening. The APA paragraph names the focal/reference
   groups, matching score, strongest MH D-DIF delta, common odds ratio,
   chi-square statistic, p values, and direction, and explicitly states that the
   route is not fitted-MFRM `RSM`, `PCM`, or bounded-`GPCM`.
5. `analyze_dff_moderation()` results are reported as continuous-covariate
   residual-moderation screening, not categorical MH DIF.
6. `dif_interaction_table()` results are reported as cell-level residual
   screening, not confirmatory operational subgroup decisions.
7. `apa_table()` accepts the differential-functioning result objects directly
   and delegates to `dif_report(..., style = "apa")`, keeping captions and notes
   synchronized.
8. `build_apa_outputs(..., dif_results = ...)` appends the conservative DIF/DFF
   paragraph, table note, caption, and section-map row without modifying the
   core MFRM reporting contract. Existing `build_apa_outputs()` calls remain
   positionally compatible because `dif_results` is a final named argument.
9. `inst/validation/dif-apa-reporting-0.2.2.R` replays the reporting adapter
   across two-level categorical DFF/DIF, three-level categorical pairwise
   DFF/DIF, continuous-covariate residual moderation, observed-score MH DIF,
   cell-level interaction screening, the refit branch, and bounded-GPCM
   screening. The release-readiness protocol runs that helper and requires an
   `ok` status before the evidence-artifacts gate passes.

## Reporting language constraints

- Use "screening-positive", "screening-negative", "large-magnitude", or
  "unclassified" according to the result object's classification system.
- Do not use "bias", "fairness", "invariance", or "operational subgroup
  decision" as conclusions unless the study supplies substantive review,
  design evidence, and an appropriate inferential basis outside the screening
  helper.
- For three-or-more-level categorical grouping variables, report the pairwise
  fitted-model DFF/DIF contrasts generated by `analyze_dff()` / `analyze_dif()`.
- For continuous person-level covariates, report
  `analyze_dff_moderation()` / `analyze_dif_moderation()` as a residual-slope
  moderation screen.
